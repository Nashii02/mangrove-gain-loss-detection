About & Frequently Asked Questions

User Guide · Aringay MENRO

A plain-language guide on how satellite imagery monitors coastal mangrove forests in Barangay Dulao, Aringay, La Union.

Why We Monitor

Mangroves protect Barangay Dulao's shoreline from storm surges, prevent coastal erosion, and serve as vital nursery grounds for local marine life. Continuous monitoring protects these natural barriers.

How the System Works

European Space Agency satellites photograph the coast every year. Our computer vision model examines these photos to automatically distinguish mangrove trees from open water and sandy beaches.

How It Helps MENRO

Instead of costly foot patrols across the entire shoreline, officials can immediately pinpoint newly grown areas or loss hotspots to deploy field personnel where they are needed most.

Understanding the Four Map Categories
● Stable Mangrove
Mangroves that were present, healthy, and green in both comparison years. These represent established, protected stands.
▲ Mapped Gain
Newly grown or naturally regenerated mangrove trees appearing where there were none in the earlier survey.
▼ Mapped Loss
Areas where mangrove canopy disappeared since the prior survey. Flags priority locations for ground investigation.
◻ Unchanged
Open sea, mudflats, and inland ground that did not have mangrove trees in either observation year.
Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Our computer model studied satellite photos across different light wavelengths—including near-infrared light, which bounces strongly off lush green foliage. Because mangroves grow directly in coastal brackish waters, their unique green reflection pattern distinguishes them from dryland plants, sandbars, and open sea.

The satellite divides the coastline into a grid of 10-meter by 10-meter squares (100 square meters each, or 0.01 hectare). To picture this, one scan block is about the size of a standard tennis court. A single hectare equals 100 such blocks, or roughly 1.4 standard soccer/football fields.
